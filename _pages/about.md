I am a PhD student in Biomedical Informatics and Data Science at Washington University in St. Louis, working in the Luna Lab at the Mallinckrodt Institute of Radiology with Dr. José Marcio Luna. My research sits at the intersection of predictive oncology, computational pathology, and cancer imaging: I build multimodal AI methods that link what we can see in tissue and on scans to the molecular biology underneath.

My current projects include:

- **H&E morphology and glycomics in triple-negative breast cancer.** Testing whether self-supervised whole-slide image embeddings capture tumor fucosylation signatures measured by MALDI mass spectrometry imaging, in a multi-institutional cohort.
- **Diffusion MRI as a window into tumor biochemistry.** Co-registering diffusion basis spectrum imaging (DBSI) with MALDI imaging in prostate cancer to ask whether non-invasive imaging tracks desmoplastic collagen remodeling across Gleason grades.
- **Pathology foundation models.** A critical review of how design choices in vision foundation models shape what they learn from H&E slides, how they transfer across clinical contexts, and where they fail.

Before WashU, I completed an M.S. in Health Informatics (with Honors) at Yale, where I worked on deep learning survival models for primary CNS lymphoma, counterfactual survival modeling for oncology trials, and large-scale EHR foundation models. I also collaborate with Yale Radiology on national studies of the U.S. radiology workforce, graduate medical education funding, and NIH funding for clinician-scientists. I received my B.Sc. in Systems Biology from the University of Hyderabad.
